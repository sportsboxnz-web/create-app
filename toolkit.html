12 Scoreboard
Ideas

One metric. One week. CREATE made visible.

How to use this pack

Pick one scoreboard per week. Write the metric on the whiteboard before the team arrives. Update it daily. Review it briefly on Friday. Change it the following week.

Two scoreboards per CREATE step — so you can rotate through the full framework in 12 weeks, or pick the ones most relevant to what you're seeing in your store right now.

Numbers on a wall change behaviour without anyone needing to say a word.

The Rule

One metric only. Keep it simple and countable. A rough tally is better than a precise number you never update.

C
Scoreboard 01
The 30-Second Greeting Tally
Connect Quickly
What you track

Number of customers greeted within 30 seconds this shift.

How to track it

Manager or nominated team member tallies every greeting observed on the floor during each shift. A simple tick on the whiteboard per greeting. Total it at end of shift.

Write on the board
Greetings within 30 seconds today: ______
Friday review
"We landed [X] greetings this week. Every single one of those customers felt welcomed the moment they walked in. Next week, let's see if we can keep every shift above [Y]."
Why it works

The greeting is the easiest CREATE behaviour to observe and the fastest to drift. Making it visible resets the standard without anyone needing to say a word.

Manager tip

Don't count greetings that miss the 30-second window — only tally the ones done right. Accuracy matters more than a high number.

C
Scoreboard 02
First Customer Response Time
Connect Quickly
What you track

How long before the first customer of the day was acknowledged.

How to track it

Manager notes the time the first customer enters and the time they were greeted. Track this each morning for the week — record the gap in seconds on the board.

Write on the board
First greeting today: ______ seconds after customer entered
Friday review
"Our average first response this week was [X] seconds. That's [better than / the same as] last week. We're aiming to keep it under 30 seconds every morning."
Why it works

The opening of the day sets the standard for the shift. Tracking it creates accountability from the first minute the door opens.

Manager tip

This one is quick to run — one observation per morning. It takes 30 seconds of manager attention to make a visible point to the whole team.

R
Scoreboard 03
Discovery Question Tracker
Really Listen
What you track

Number of times the project discovery question was used this shift.

How to track it

Manager listens for the exact phrase "Tell me about the project you're working on" during floor walks. Tally each time it's used correctly. One tally mark per interaction observed.

Write on the board
Discovery questions asked today: ______
Friday review
"We asked the discovery question [X] times this week across the interactions I observed. That means [X] customers felt heard before we recommended anything. Let's make it automatic."
Why it works

Advice given before listening feels like a sales pitch. Tracking the discovery question makes listening a visible, measurable behaviour — not just an expectation.

Manager tip

Only count the exact phrase or a clear equivalent — not "Can I help you." The specificity is the point.

R
Scoreboard 04
Project Confirmed Counter
Really Listen
What you track

Number of interactions where the team member confirmed understanding before recommending.

How to track it

During floor walks, listen for the confirmation step — "So if I've got this right…" or "Just to confirm…" Tally each time a team member reflects back what they've heard before moving to products.

Write on the board
Confirmations before recommendations today: ______
Friday review
"We confirmed understanding [X] times before making recommendations this week. That's [X] customers who felt properly listened to. Next week let's make it a habit, not a highlight."
Why it works

Most staff ask the discovery question but skip the confirmation. Tracking confirmation closes the loop — it proves the listening actually happened.

Manager tip

This one's slightly harder to observe than the greeting. Use it in weeks when you're doing regular floor walks and can listen to full interactions.

E
Scoreboard 05
Plain Language Wins
Explain Simply
What you track

Number of times a technical term was translated into plain language this shift.

How to track it

During floor observations, listen for the translation moment — "[term], which means [benefit]." Tally each time a team member explains a feature in customer-friendly language unprompted.

Write on the board
Plain language explanations today: ______
Friday review
"We caught [X] plain language moments this week. Each one of those customers left feeling confident rather than confused. Confusion delays decisions — clarity closes them."
Why it works

Technical dumping is the default for people who know their product well. Making plain language visible rewards the habit of translating specs into benefits.

Manager tip

Praise specific examples in your Friday call-out — name the team member and what they said. This scoreboard works best paired with the Floor Walk weapon.

E
Scoreboard 06
Three Options Rule
Explain Simply
What you track

Number of interactions where the team member offered two or three options — not more.

How to track it

Observe product selection conversations. Tally each time a team member narrows choice to two or three clear options with a recommendation, rather than showing an overwhelming range.

Write on the board
Curated options presented today: ______
Friday review
"We gave customers a curated choice [X] times this week. Fewer options, better decisions. Next week let's make sure every product conversation ends with a clear recommendation."
Why it works

Showing 20 options creates decision fatigue. Two or three curated choices with a recommendation builds customer confidence and shortens the sales cycle.

Manager tip

This is a coaching signal too — if the number is low, run a quick Bad vs Best demo in Monday's micro install showing the difference between overwhelming and curating.

A
Scoreboard 07
Website Quote Counter
Add Value
What you track

Number of website quotes generated this shift.

How to track it

Simple tally of quotes generated through the Azul website quote function each shift. Count is pulled from the system at end of day or tallied manually as quotes are created.

Write on the board
Website quotes generated today: ______  |  Week total: ______
Friday review
"We generated [X] website quotes this week. Every one of those customers left with a professional, visual quote they can share with their partner, builder, or family. That's [X] chances to win the sale after they leave."
Why it works

The website quote is the single most trackable Add Value behaviour. It either happened or it didn't. Zero tolerance for manual quotes starts with making the number visible.

Manager tip

This is the strongest scoreboard for Add Value — run it any time you see manual quotes creeping back in. The number on the board speaks louder than any conversation.

A
Scoreboard 08
Full Project Conversations
Add Value
What you track

Number of interactions where a complementary product or category was mentioned.

How to track it

During floor walks and counter observations, tally each time a team member proactively mentions bathware, laminate, maintenance products, trade supplies, or any category beyond what the customer asked for.

Write on the board
Full project conversations today: ______
Friday review
"We had [X] full project conversations this week — customers who heard about more than just what they came in for. That's [X] opportunities to be their one stop shop."
Why it works

Most customers don't know the full range Tile Depot carries. Tracking full project conversations builds the habit of thinking beyond the immediate request.

Manager tip

Pair this with a Monday micro install — spend two minutes showing the team how to bridge from tiles to bathware naturally: "Since you're doing the whole bathroom, have you thought about your vanity?"

T
Scoreboard 09
Quote Read-Back Tracker
Take Responsibility
What you track

Number of complete quote read-backs performed at the counter this shift.

How to track it

Manager or senior team member observes counter interactions. Tally each time a full read-back is completed — tile name, size, details, quantity, availability, and guarantee all mentioned before the customer leaves.

Write on the board
Complete read-backs today: ______
Friday review
"We completed [X] full read-backs this week. Every one of those is a potential error caught before installation day. Next week let's make sure the number matches the number of quotes processed."
Why it works

The read-back is the most frequently skipped non-negotiable. Tracking it makes the skipping visible without the manager having to say anything directly.

Manager tip

Compare this number to the website quote count — ideally they should be similar. A big gap between quotes generated and read-backs completed tells you exactly where to focus coaching.

T
Scoreboard 10
Guarantee Mentions
Take Responsibility
What you track

Number of times the 10-year guarantee was mentioned during a quote or counter interaction.

How to track it

Tally each time a team member mentions the 10-year quality guarantee during a counter interaction. Can be self-reported at end of shift or observed during floor walks.

Write on the board
Guarantee mentions today: ______
Friday review
"We mentioned the guarantee [X] times this week. Every customer who heard it left with one less reason to shop elsewhere. The guarantee removes purchase anxiety — use it."
Why it works

The guarantee is a genuine differentiator and a confidence builder for uncertain customers. Tracking it turns it from background knowledge into an active sales tool.

Manager tip

This one works well as a self-reported scoreboard — ask team members to tally their own mentions at end of shift. It builds self-awareness without feeling like surveillance.

End Memorably

Non-NegotiableAlways thank by name, reference their project, and invite them back.

E
Scoreboard 11
Personalised Goodbye Counter
End Memorably
What you track

Number of closing interactions that included the customer's name or a project reference.

How to track it

During floor walks near the counter, listen for the close. Tally each time a team member references the customer's project, uses their name, or issues a genuine invitation to return — rather than a generic "Thanks, have a good day."

Write on the board
Memorable closings today: ______
Friday review
"We gave [X] personalised goodbyes this week. Those customers didn't just have a transaction — they had an experience. That's what brings them back and what they tell their friends about."
Why it works

The close is the last thing a customer remembers. Research shows endings disproportionately shape the overall memory of an experience. Making memorable closes visible raises the standard without a lecture.

Manager tip

At Friday's recognition moment, share one specific closing line a team member used this week — quoting it exactly. Real examples spread faster than instructions.

E
Scoreboard 12
Come Back Invitations
End Memorably
What you track

Number of times a customer was specifically invited to return or show photos of their finished project.

How to track it

Tally each time a team member closes with a genuine invitation — "Come back and show us how it turned out" or "Let us know how the installation goes." Self-reported at end of shift works well for this one.

Write on the board
Return invitations today: ______
Friday review
"We invited [X] customers back this week. That's not a sales tactic — it's genuine interest in their project. Customers who feel cared for beyond the transaction become the customers who refer their friends."
Why it works

Inviting customers back signals care rather than transaction. It's the simplest way to turn a one-time purchase into a long-term relationship — and it costs nothing.

Manager tip

Run this scoreboard in a week where you're also using the WhatsApp Nudge on End Memorably — the two reinforce each other and you'll see the number climb by mid-week.
